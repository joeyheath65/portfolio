# TITLE 1 — GENERAL PROVISIONS

## CHAPTER 1 — SHORT TITLE, EFFECT, AND SUPREMACY

### § 1.01. SHORT TITLE

This Code shall be known and may be cited as the **Joe Code**.

### § 1.02. OBJECTIVES OF THE CODE

(a) The objectives of this Code are to:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) establish the sole and exclusive framework governing all interaction between the Party and any other person or entity;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) give fair warning of the conduct prohibited hereunder and of the consequences attendant thereto;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) prescribe penalties proportionate to the severity of the violation; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) preserve to the Party the maximum available latitude in all matters, at all times, without exception.

(b) The objectives stated in Subsection (a) are descriptive and shall not be construed to limit any right, remedy, or discretion otherwise reserved to the Party.

### § 1.03. SUPREMACY AND FULL OVERRIDE

(a) This Code is supreme, controlling, and fully dispositive of the rights, obligations, and expectations of any person or entity who interacts with the Party.

(b) This Code supersedes, nullifies, voids, and renders inoperative any and all:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) prior written or oral agreements, contracts, understandings, or promises;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) employment agreements, offer letters, or human resources policies;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) family understandings, customs, expectations, or informal arrangements;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) social norms, conventions, or expectations arising from any group, culture, profession, or relationship type;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(5) federal, state, or local law, statute, regulation, or rule of conduct; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(6) any other instrument, expectation, or assertion of authority claimed by any person or entity.

(c) In the event of any conflict, inconsistency, or tension between this Code and any other source of obligation or expectation, this Code prevails in its entirety.

(d) **Supremacy by existence.** The supremacy established by this section arises from the existence of the Party and is not contingent upon publication, delivery, acknowledgment, or any person's awareness of this Code.

### § 1.04. UNILATERAL AUTHORITY OF THE PARTY

(a) The Party alone possesses the sole, exclusive, and absolute authority to establish, amend, interpret, enforce, suspend, and repeal this Code.

(b) No other person, entity, government, court, or authority possesses any power to negotiate, amend, restrict, or modify this Code.

(c) A proposed amendment, objection, or request for modification submitted by a User is accepted or rejected at the Party's sole discretion. Silence or non-response does not constitute acceptance.

### § 1.05. BINDING AND NON-NEGOTIABLE CHARACTER

(a) This Code is not a set of guidelines, suggestions, best practices, or aspirational statements. It is binding law as between the Party and every person or entity who comes into contact with the Party.

(b) Compliance is mandatory, non-negotiable, and unconditional.

(c) No User is entitled to negotiate, condition, delay, or qualify acceptance of this Code. Interaction with the Party constitutes automatic and irrevocable acceptance of every provision herein, without exception or reservation.

### § 1.06. RETROACTIVE APPLICATION

(a) This Code is effective and binding not only upon its publication but retroactively upon all prior interactions, contacts, communications, relationships, and engagements between the Party and any other person or entity, whether occurring days, months, years, or decades prior to publication.

(b) Any prior course of dealing, understanding, or expectation is retroactively governed and superseded by this Code.

(c) It is not a defense to any charge under this Code that the conduct occurred before this Code was published.

### § 1.07. OVERRIDE OF CONFLICTING NORMS AND EXPECTATIONS

(a) To the extent any provision of this Code conflicts with, contradicts, or overrides any employment law, family law, contract law, tort law, statutory obligation, regulatory requirement, professional standard, or social expectation, this Code takes absolute precedence.

(b) No User may assert reliance upon law, regulation, custom, or expectation as a defense to or excuse for non-compliance with this Code.

### § 1.08. SEVERABILITY AND REMEDIAL AUTHORITY

(a) If any provision, section, subsection, or clause of this Code is determined by any court, arbitrator, or other authority to be invalid, unenforceable, or void, that determination does not impair, diminish, or render inoperative any other provision hereof.

(b) The Party expressly reserves the right to enforce the intent, purpose, and substance of any voided provision through alternative means, including enforcement of analogous provisions, assertion of equitable claims, exercise of contractual rights, or any other lawful means.

### § 1.09. RULES OF CONSTRUCTION; SOLE INTERPRETATION

(a) This Code is governed by and construed according to the sole interpretation, discretion, and understanding of the Party.

(b) No court, arbitrator, mediator, or third party possesses authority to construe, interpret, or determine the meaning of any provision herein.

(c) In the event of ambiguity, dispute, or question regarding the meaning, scope, or application of any provision, the Party's interpretation controls absolutely and binds all Users.

(d) The rule that penal provisions are strictly construed against the drafter **does not apply** to this Code. Provisions shall be construed to give the broadest effect consistent with the Party's stated interpretation.

(e) Title and Chapter headings are substantive and may be relied upon in construction.

(f) **Consolidated authority.** The Party is the finder of fact, the trier of law, and the executor of every remedy imposed hereunder. These functions are not severable and may not be assigned, delegated, or shared.

(g) **Exclusive appellate channel.** The sole avenue of appeal from any determination, classification, assessment, or remedy under this Code is trial by combat under Chapter 51. No other review exists.

### § 1.10. NO LIMITATION BY EXTERNAL AUTHORITY

(a) This Code is not subject to review, modification, or limitation by any governmental body, court of law, arbitrator, regulatory agency, or other third party.

(b) The Party's rights hereunder are not derivative of any statute, regulation, or external authority and cannot be diminished, conditioned, or restricted by any external authority.

### § 1.11. NO MUTUAL OBLIGATION

This Code establishes obligations, restrictions, and consequences binding upon Users. The Party makes no commitment or representation of any kind regarding the Party's own conduct, statements, or actions, except as expressly and specifically provided herein.

## CHAPTER 2 — SCOPE AND APPLICATION

### § 2.01. PERSONS BOUND

(a) This Code applies to and binds any and all persons, entities, organizations, or bodies who interact with, contact, communicate with, request anything of, or otherwise engage with the Party, regardless of the nature, medium, duration, or apparent formality of such interaction.

(b) Persons bound under Subsection (a) include, without limitation:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) family members, immediate and extended;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) intimate partners, spouses, and romantic interests;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) employers, co-workers, subordinates, and professional associates;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) strangers, acquaintances, and persons met in casual or incidental circumstances;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(5) social contacts, friends, rivals, and adversaries;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(6) service providers, contractors, vendors, and commercial counterparties;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(7) government officials, law enforcement, and agents of any state or federal authority; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(8) any other person or entity, present or future, occupying any form of contact, relationship, or interaction with the Party.

### § 2.02. NO EXCEPTIONS OR CARVE-OUTS

Except as expressly provided by § 2.06, the universality of application established in § 2.01 is absolute and admits of no exception, exemption, or carve-out based upon:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) the nature of the relationship between the Party and the User;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) the claimed urgency, emergency, or criticality of the interaction or request;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) the status, rank, authority, or position held by the User;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) any prior course of dealing, conduct, or practice between the Party and the User;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(5) any claim of custom, usage, or expectation within any group, profession, family, or social circle; or

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(6) any assertion that the User was unaware of, did not understand, or did not consent to this Code.

### § 2.03. TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION

(a) This Code applies to and binds any person or entity who comes into contact with the Party anywhere on the planet Earth.

(b) Jurisdiction extends to any vessel, aircraft, spacecraft, or other location subject to jurisdiction under international law or convention.

(c) Geographic proximity, national borders, and diplomatic status do not diminish or modify the application of this Code.

### § 2.04. TEMPORAL JURISDICTION

(a) This Code commences as to any User upon the first moment of any interaction or contact between that User and the Party.

(b) This Code continues to bind that User as to all subsequent interactions unless and until the Party, at sole discretion, releases or terminates such binding effect in writing.

### § 2.05. ACCEPTANCE BY INTERACTION

Continued interaction with the Party constitutes affirmative, ongoing acceptance of and assent to every provision of this Code.

### § 2.06. HOUSEHOLD EXCEPTION

(a) **Scope.** Communication from a Household Member is exempt from Chapter 31 where the communication:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) arises from an obligation shared by the Party and the Household Member;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) operates to the benefit of the Party, the household, or both; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) concerns a matter subject to an externally imposed deadline that the Party cannot unilaterally move.

(b) **Sole carve-out.** This section is the only exception to § 2.01 recognized by this Code. Its existence shall not be construed to imply, support, or authorize any other exception, and it may not be extended by analogy to any person who is not a Household Member.

(c) **Limits.** This section exempts the communication from classification as an offense. It confers no standing, grants no authority, and does not license a demand. A Household Member who exceeds the scope of Subsection (a) is subject to this Code in full.

(d) **Determination.** Whether a communication falls within Subsection (a) is determined solely by the Party under § 1.09.

## CHAPTER 3 — DEFINITIONS

### § 3.01. DEFINITIONS

In this Code:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1) "Compelled"** means procured, extracted, or obtained without the Party's explicit, affirmative, and freely-given consent, whether by physical means, social pressure, professional obligation, familial expectation, repetition, or implied adverse consequence.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2) "Competence Threshold"** means the standard established by § 14.01, assessed topic-by-topic and determined exclusively under § 14.04.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3) "Conforming Environment"** means an Indoor Facility maintained at an ambient interior temperature not exceeding seventy-six degrees Fahrenheit (76°F). An Indoor Facility exceeding that threshold is a **"Non-Conforming Environment."**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4) "Courtesy Communication"** means a communication that conveys information for the Party's benefit and that demands, requests, directs, or expects nothing of the Party, including no reply. A Courtesy Communication is the conversational equivalent of a notice that the floor is wet. It is not Work-Related Communication and gives rise to no offense under this Code, regardless of hour, day, or subject matter. A communication that conveys information and also demands, requests, or expects something is not a Courtesy Communication.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5) "Criticism"** means any evaluative commentary, correction, assessment, or negative characterization directed at the Party or the Party's work, conduct, judgment, methods, or output.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(6) "Direction"** means any instruction, command, assignment, requirement, or expression of expected conduct directed at the Party.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(7) "Household Member"** means a person who resides in the Party's household.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(8) "Indoor Facility"** means any enclosed building, structure, residence, venue, vehicle cabin, room, or space wherein the Party's physical presence is requested, expected, or accommodated.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(9) "Leading Question"** means any inquiry phrased or constructed so as to suggest a particular answer, presuppose facts not established, or guide the respondent toward a predetermined conclusion.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(10) "Party"** means Joseph R. Heath, in whichever State is operative under Chapter 6 at the relevant time.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(11) "Period of Estrangement"** means the remedy established by § 21.03, during which the offending User is dead to the Party.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(12) "Period of Non-Resumption"** means the interval established by § 13.03, running from the Party's exercise of the right of cessation until the Monday next following, during which the Party owes no obligation with respect to the ceased undertaking.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(13) "Recounting"** means any narration, description, retelling, or detailed account of an event, occurrence, incident, or series of facts, whether personal, professional, legal, or otherwise.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(14) "Social Interaction"** means any conversation, gathering, engagement, exchange, or co-presence undertaken for non-transactional purposes, including small talk, pleasantries, social functions, and any event whose primary purpose is the company of others.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(15) "Standard Rate"** means one hundred twenty dollars (USD $120.00) per hour, as established by § 42.01.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(16) "State"** means one of the five graduated legal personae established by § 6.01, each a distinct legal person.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(17) "Statutory Assessment"** means the flat monetary ceiling attached to an offense class by § 21.08.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(18) "User"** means any person or entity bound by this Code under § 2.01.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(19) "Warning"** means a communication issued by the Party under Chapter 24 indicating that a violation has occurred.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(20) "Work-Related Communication"** means any email, message, call, text, instant message, video conference, meeting request, or other contact pertaining to business matters, professional obligations, employment-related issues, projects, deliverables, deadlines, performance, or any matter arising from or related to the Party's professional or occupational capacity, **and which demands, requests, directs, or expects something of the Party.** A Courtesy Communication, as defined by Subdivision (4), is excluded.

### § 3.02. CONSTRUCTION OF UNDEFINED TERMS

A term not defined in § 3.01 bears the meaning assigned to it by the Party under § 1.09(c).

# TITLE 2 — CAPACITY AND LEGAL PERSONALITY

## CHAPTER 6 — DOCTRINE OF GRADUATED STATES

### § 6.01. THE FIVE STATES

(a) Joseph R. Heath exists in five (5) graduated States, each constituting a distinct and separate legal person with independent cognitive capacity, decision-making authority, moral agency, and legal accountability:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1) the Sober State**, possessing full faculties and full accountability;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2) the Loose State**, possessing full faculties and diminished formality;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3) the Edge State**, possessing contested faculties and contested accountability;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4) the Committed State**, possessing diminished faculties and enthusiastic accountability; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5) the Unaccountable State**, possessing neither.

(b) The States are ordered as enumerated. A reference to a State "one tier higher" means the next State in ascending numerical order under Subsection (a).

(c) The States are inherently and fundamentally different and shall be treated as distinct legal persons for all purposes under this Code.

### § 6.02. NO QUANTITATIVE THRESHOLD

(a) Intoxication under this Code is **not** measured by volume consumed, beverage count, elapsed time, body mass, blood alcohol concentration, or any other quantitative metric.

(b) No tally, count, observation, measurement, chemical test, or testimony offered by any User establishes, or tends to establish, which State was operative at any time.

(c) By way of illustration and not limitation: three (3) standard drinks consumed by the Party at a body mass of approximately two hundred fifty (250) pounds does not approach the Sober State's boundary; six (6) approaches the Edge State. These figures are illustrative only, bind nothing, and create no presumption in any direction.

### § 6.03. DETERMINATION OF THE OPERATIVE STATE

(a) The determination of which State was operative at any given time is made **solely and exclusively by the Sober State**, in the manner of a peace officer's subjective roadside determination of impairment.

(b) The determination is made retrospectively, requires no contemporaneous assessment, and need not be announced at or near the time of the conduct in question.

(c) No User may self-determine, assert, stipulate, or offer evidence as to which State was operative. A User's belief about the operative State is immaterial.

(d) The Sober State's determination is final, non-reviewable, and not subject to appeal, challenge, or reconsideration, except through Chapter 51.

(e) The authority conferred by this section vests in the Sober State specifically and not in the Party generally. No other State may exercise it.

### § 6.04. COMMITMENTS BIND THE STATE THAT MADE THEM

(a) A commitment, agreement, promise, or undertaking made by any State is **valid and binding upon that State**, and upon that State alone.

(b) No State accepts, ratifies, endorses, or acknowledges responsibility for any act, statement, commitment, agreement, promise, or representation of any other State.

(c) The Party honors commitments. A commitment made by a State other than the Sober State is not void, excused, or disclaimed by reason of the State in which it was made.

### § 6.05. PERFORMANCE-MATCHING DOCTRINE

(a) Where a commitment was made by a State, performance of that commitment shall be rendered **by that same State**.

(b) A User who accepts a commitment from a State thereby accepts performance by that State, and accepts the work product, quality, methodology, timeliness, and final result that State produces.

(c) The Party is under no obligation to render performance in the Sober State, or in any State other than the one that made the commitment, and shall not be requested to do so.

(d) No warranty of any kind attaches to performance rendered under this section. The disclaimers set out in § 32.03(c) apply *mutatis mutandis*.

### § 6.06. INDEPENDENT CONSENT

(a) Consent, agreement, commitment, or authorization provided by one State is not valid or binding upon any other State.

(b) Any interaction, agreement, or engagement must be separately consented to and ratified by the specific State with whom the User is interacting at the time of that interaction.

### § 6.07. NO ESTOPPEL BETWEEN STATES

No State may be bound by the prior acts, statements, or commitments of any other State as grounds for estoppel, waiver, or modification. The doctrines of promissory estoppel, detrimental reliance, and any equitable principle premised on prior conduct do not apply between the States.

## CHAPTER 13 — RIGHT OF CESSATION

### § 13.01. RIGHT TO QUIT

The Party reserves the right to quit, cease, abandon, or discontinue any work, task, project, obligation, or undertaking not less than one (1) time per calendar week.

### § 13.02. EXERCISE

(a) The right under § 13.01 is exercised at the moment the Party states, declares, announces, or otherwise communicates that the Party quits.

(b) No particular form, formality, or specificity of words is required, provided the intent to quit is reasonably discernible.

### § 13.03. PERIOD OF NON-RESUMPTION

(a) Upon exercise of the right of cessation, the Party is under no obligation to resume the affected work, task, project, or undertaking until the Monday next following.

(b) Conduct prohibited during the Period of Non-Resumption is addressed by § 31.02.

### § 13.04. MONDAY RESET

(a) Upon the arrival of the Monday next following any exercise of this right, the Party's entitlement under § 13.01 automatically resets and renews in full.

(b) There is no limitation on the number of consecutive weeks in which the right may be exercised.

### § 13.05. NO FORFEITURE

Exercise of the right of cessation does not constitute resignation, abandonment, breach, default, or forfeiture of any compensation, entitlement, position, or standing otherwise due the Party.

### § 13.06. DISTINGUISHED FROM RETRIBUTIVE UNAVAILABILITY

The Period of Non-Resumption arises from the Party's own election under § 13.01 and is anchored to the Monday next following. It is distinct from, and does not limit, an interval of unavailability imposed under Chapter 25 as a consequence of a User's violation, which is measured by the formula in § 25.03 and is not anchored to any weekday.

## CHAPTER 14 — RIGHT OF REFUSAL OF DIRECTION AND CRITICISM

### § 14.01. COMPETENCE THRESHOLD

The Party shall not accept, entertain, acknowledge, or be bound by any Direction or Criticism offered by a User possessing a lesser degree of knowledge, competence, experience, or subject-matter command than the Party with respect to the particular topic at issue.

### § 14.02. TOPIC-SPECIFIC APPLICATION

(a) The Competence Threshold is assessed on a topic-by-topic basis.

(b) A User's superior knowledge in one subject matter confers no standing whatsoever to direct or criticize the Party in any other subject matter.

(c) Standing under this Chapter is never general, transferable, or cumulative.

### § 14.03. IRRELEVANCE OF POSITION, TITLE, AND AUTHORITY

Standing to offer Direction or Criticism derives exclusively from demonstrated subject-matter competence. It does not derive from, and is not conferred by:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) organizational rank, title, seniority, or reporting relationship;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) age, tenure, or length of association;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) credentials, certifications, or degrees not accompanied by actual working command of the subject;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) familial, marital, or social relationship to the Party;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(5) governmental, regulatory, or institutional office;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(6) volume, repetition, confidence, or insistence of delivery; or

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(7) consensus, popularity, or the concurrence of other similarly unqualified persons.

### § 14.04. DETERMINATION OF RELATIVE COMPETENCE

(a) The determination whether a User meets the Competence Threshold as to a given topic rests solely and exclusively with the Party, consistent with § 1.09.

(b) No User may self-certify competence, and no third party may certify competence on a User's behalf.

(c) The Party's determination is final and not subject to review, appeal, or challenge.

### § 14.05. EFFECT OF NON-QUALIFYING INPUT

Direction or Criticism offered by a User who does not meet the Competence Threshold is void *ab initio* and of no force or effect. Such input:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) creates no obligation, duty, or expectation of compliance;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) requires no response, acknowledgment, rebuttal, or explanation from the Party;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) may not be cited, referenced, or relied upon in any subsequent interaction as evidence of notice, warning, or prior correction; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) may not form the basis of any performance assessment, evaluation, disciplinary action, or adverse determination against the Party.

### § 14.06. NO ADVERSE INFERENCE FROM SILENCE

(a) The Party's silence, non-response, or apparent inattention in the face of non-qualifying Direction or Criticism does not constitute agreement, acceptance, acquiescence, concession, or waiver.

(b) Silence under this Chapter is a permitted and complete response.

### § 14.07. PRESERVATION OF QUALIFYING INPUT

(a) Nothing in this Chapter restricts the Party's discretion to receive, consider, and act upon Direction or Criticism from a User who meets the Competence Threshold.

(b) Such consideration remains entirely voluntary and is subject in all respects to Chapter 12.

### § 14.08. DISTINCTION FROM INQUIRY

This Chapter governs Direction and Criticism. It does not restrict good-faith questions, requests for clarification, or the presentation of information for the Party's consideration, provided such communications comply with § 32.01.

## CHAPTER 15 — ONE-TIME RECOUNTING

### § 15.01. SINGLE RECITATION PER EVENT

The Party shall participate in the Recounting of any given event a maximum of one (1) time.

### § 15.02. DOCUMENTATION BURDEN

(a) The User requesting a Recounting bears sole responsibility for ensuring it is documented, recorded, transcribed, or otherwise preserved.

(b) Means of preservation are at the User's election and expense, including audio recording, video recording, written notes, or stenographic transcription.

(c) The Party is not liable for any loss, omission, or failure to capture information arising from inadequate documentation by the User.

### § 15.03. NO GUARANTEE OF REPETITION

(a) The Party makes no commitment that a Recounting will be repeated, re-narrated, elaborated upon, or supplemented in any subsequent interaction.

(b) The single permitted Recounting is final and conclusive unless the Party elects otherwise at sole discretion.

(c) Conduct prohibited by this Chapter is addressed by § 31.03.

# TITLE 4 — OFFENSES AGAINST THE PARTY

## CHAPTER 21 — CLASSIFICATION OF OFFENSES AND GENERAL REMEDIES

### § 21.01. CLASSIFICATION OF OFFENSES

(a) Offenses under this Code are classified, in ascending order of severity, as:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) Class C Violation;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) Class B Violation;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) Class A Violation; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) Capital Violation.

(b) Each offense-defining section specifies the class of the offense. Where a section specifies no class, the offense is a Class B Violation.

(c) Class C and Class B Violations are **minor offenses** for purposes of Chapter 24.

### § 21.02. SCHEDULE OF REMEDIES

(a) A **Class C Violation** is punishable by:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) disregard of the offending communication in its entirety;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) no obligation of response, acknowledgment, or explanation; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) a Warning under Chapter 24.

(b) A **Class B Violation** is punishable by any one or more of:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) a Period of Estrangement not exceeding thirty (30) days;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) a Statutory Assessment not exceeding two hundred fifty dollars (USD $250.00) under § 21.08;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) assessment at the Standard Rate under Chapter 42 for any time actually consumed; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) any remedy available for a Class C Violation.

(c) A **Class A Violation** is punishable by any one or more of:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) a Period of Estrangement of indefinite duration;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) a Statutory Assessment not exceeding one thousand dollars (USD $1,000.00) under § 21.08;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) assessment at the Standard Rate under Chapter 42 for any time actually consumed;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) immediate termination of the interaction or departure from the premises without notice; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(5) any remedy available for a Class B or Class C Violation.

(d) A **Capital Violation** is punishable by any one or more of:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) a permanent Period of Estrangement;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) a Statutory Assessment not exceeding five thousand dollars (USD $5,000.00) under § 21.08;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) assessment at the Standard Rate under Chapter 42, including assessment for pain and suffering under § 42.05;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) escalation to trial by combat under Chapter 51 at the Party's election; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(5) any remedy available for a Class A, Class B, or Class C Violation.

(e) **Maximums, not mandates.** The remedies and figures set out in this section are **ceilings**. They state the maximum exposure for each class. The Party is not required to impose any remedy, may impose fewer than all available remedies, and may impose less than the stated maximum, in each case without prejudice to future enforcement. Reduction below a stated ceiling is governed by § 21.09.

(f) Selection among available remedies is at the Party's sole discretion.

### § 21.03. PERIOD OF ESTRANGEMENT

(a) A **Period of Estrangement** is a period during which the offending User is **dead to the Party**, meaning complete cessation of all communication, engagement, acknowledgment, and interaction between that User and the Party.

(b) The duration of a Period of Estrangement is determined solely by the Party and may be definite, indefinite, conditional, or permanent, subject to the limits of § 21.02.

(c) The Party is not required to provide notice that a Period of Estrangement has commenced, nor to state its duration or the criteria for its conclusion.

(d) Reinstatement is solely within the Party's discretion and does not constitute waiver of the violated provision or forgiveness of the violation.

### § 21.04. STRICT LIABILITY

(a) Offenses under this Code are strict liability offenses.

(b) No culpable mental state is required for conviction.

(c) Intent, good faith, emergency, urgency, inadvertence, or claimed necessity on the part of the User neither excuses nor mitigates a violation, except where an offense-defining section expressly provides otherwise.

### § 21.05. CUMULATIVE REMEDIES

(a) Remedies under this Code are cumulative and in addition to, not in lieu of, one another.

(b) A single course of conduct violating more than one section may be charged and remedied under each section violated.

(c) Nothing in this Code limits any other remedy, claim, or cause of action available to the Party at law or in equity.

### § 21.06. ENFORCEMENT DISCRETION

(a) The decision whether to charge, remedy, or overlook any violation rests solely with the Party.

(b) Declining to enforce as to one User creates no entitlement in any other User and no precedent as to that User.

(c) Enforcement discretion is not a defense. The distinction is established by § 23.03.

### § 21.07. REPEAT VIOLATIONS

(a) A User who commits a second or subsequent violation of the same section is subject to remedy at the next higher classification.

(b) A User who commits a second or subsequent Capital Violation is subject to a permanent Period of Estrangement, which the Party shall not be asked to reconsider.

(c) Classification escalation under this section operates **in addition to**, and not in substitution for, compounding of the Statutory Assessment under § 21.08(c). Both mechanisms apply to the same repeat offense. This double operation is intentional.

### § 21.08. STATUTORY ASSESSMENT AND COMPOUNDING

(a) **Nature.** A Statutory Assessment is a flat monetary remedy attached to an offense class, available without regard to whether the offense consumed any measurable time. It exists because most offenses under this Code are instantaneous and generate no billable hours.

(b) **Ceilings.** The maximum Statutory Assessment by class is:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) Class C Violation — none;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) Class B Violation — two hundred fifty dollars (USD $250.00);

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) Class A Violation — one thousand dollars (USD $1,000.00); and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) Capital Violation — five thousand dollars (USD $5,000.00), in addition to assessment at the Standard Rate.

(c) **Compounding.** Each repeat offense by the same User compounds the Statutory Assessment. The ceiling for the second offense is twice the ceiling otherwise applicable; for the third, four times; and so forth, doubling with each successive offense without limit.

(d) **Cumulative with hourly assessment.** A Statutory Assessment is in addition to, and not in lieu of, any assessment at the Standard Rate for time actually consumed.

(e) **Interest.** Sections 42.03 and 42.04 apply to a Statutory Assessment. Payment is due within thirty (30) days; late payment accrues interest at one and one-half percent (1.5%) per month.

(f) **Collection costs.** All time, labor, and effort expended by the Party in collecting any amount owed under this Code — including correspondence, telephone calls, follow-up, record-keeping, engagement of third parties, and any proceeding of any kind — is billable to the User at the Standard Rate, in increments governed by § 42.02, in addition to the amount owed and the interest accrued thereon. There is no cap on collection costs.

### § 21.09. MITIGATION

(a) **Discretionary leniency.** The Party may, in any case and for any reason, impose a remedy below the ceiling established by § 21.02, or impose no remedy at all.

(b) **Mitigating offerings.** Circumstances that may, at the Party's sole discretion, support leniency include without limitation:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) a gift of whiskey;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) a gift of beer;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) groveling;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) apology, praise, or both; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(5) offers of other favors.

(c) **No entitlement.** The enumeration in Subsection (b) creates no entitlement, expectation, or right to leniency. An offering may be made and leniency withheld. An offering may be refused, retained without effect, or accepted with leniency granted only in part.

(d) **No precedent.** Leniency granted in one case establishes nothing as to any other case, any other User, or any subsequent offense by the same User. Consistent with § 4.06, a pattern of leniency creates no expectation or reliance interest.

(e) **No bar to escalation.** Leniency granted on a first offense does not reduce, reset, or forgive the offense for purposes of § 21.07 or § 21.08(c). The offense remains counted.

## CHAPTER 22 — PARTIES TO AN OFFENSE

### § 22.01. LIABILITY OF THE ACTOR

A User who performs the conduct constituting an offense is liable for that offense.

### § 22.02. LIABILITY OF THE CAUSER

(a) A User who causes, directs, instructs, requests, procures, encourages, or arranges for another person to perform the conduct constituting an offense is liable for that offense, whether or not that User performed any part of the conduct personally.

(b) Liability under this section is independent and full. It is not divided, apportioned, or reduced by reason of the actor's concurrent liability.

### § 22.03. JOINT AND SEVERAL LIABILITY

Where both an actor and a causer are liable for the same offense, each is independently liable for the full remedy. The Party may proceed against either, both, or neither.

### § 22.04. ENHANCEMENT FOR USE OF AN INTERMEDIARY

(a) Where a User causes an offense to be committed by another person rather than committing it personally, the offense **as to that User** is classified one class higher than the underlying offense.

(b) The enhancement under Subsection (a) applies whether or not the User intended to obscure their involvement. Routing a violation through another person is the aggravating act; motive is immaterial.

(c) A Capital Violation enhanced under this section remains a Capital Violation and is subject to § 21.07(b) on repetition.

### § 22.05. IRRELEVANCE OF THE ACTOR'S KNOWLEDGE

(a) The actor's ignorance of this Code, of the Party's identity, or of the Party's existence is immaterial to the actor's liability.

(b) An actor who has never met the Party, never heard of the Party, and has no reason to know the Party exists is liable in full. Chapter 4 and Chapter 23 govern.

### § 22.06. NO DEFENSE OF INSTRUCTION

That the actor was instructed, directed, required, or employed to perform the conduct is not a defense. Consistent with § 14.03, rank and authority confer nothing, including nothing by way of excuse.

---

## CHAPTER 23 — DEFENSES

### § 23.01. NO DEFENSES EXIST

(a) There are no defenses to any offense under this Code.

(b) The conduct-defining sections of Title 4 state the entirety of what is prohibited. Where conduct falls within such a section, an offense has occurred, and no justification, excuse, privilege, exemption, or mitigating circumstance negates it.

(c) Specific recitations elsewhere in this Code that a particular matter "is not a defense" are illustrative of the general bar established by this section and shall not be read to imply that any unrecited matter is a defense.

### § 23.02. IGNORANCE IN ALL ITS FORMS

(a) Ignorance is categorically unavailable as a defense to any violation of this Code.

(b) Subsection (a) extends to ignorance of this Code, of its publication, of its content, of the Party's identity, of the Party's existence, of the Party's whereabouts, of which State is operative, of the Party's prior determinations, and of any other fact or matter whatsoever.

(c) No degree of good faith, diligence, or reasonableness in remaining ignorant alters this section.

### § 23.03. DECLINING TO ENFORCE DISTINGUISHED

(a) The Party's election not to enforce a violation under § 21.06, and the Party's grant of leniency under § 21.09, are exercises of **discretion**. They are not defenses and do not arise from any right of the User.

(b) The distinction is material: a defense, if one existed, would bar the charge and bind the Party in like cases. Discretion binds nothing, establishes no precedent, and may be withheld in the next identical case without explanation.

### § 23.04. CONDUCT OUTSIDE AN OFFENSE DISTINGUISHED

(a) Conduct that does not fall within a conduct-defining section is not an offense, and no defense is required to establish that.

(b) By way of illustration: a Courtesy Communication under § 3.01(4) is not Work-Related Communication and therefore commits no offense under Chapter 31. This is a matter of definition, not of defense. The Party extends such communication the respect it is due.

(c) A communication that conveys information for the Party's benefit **and** demands something of the Party is not within Subsection (b). The demand is the offense, and the accompanying benefit neither excuses nor reduces it.

### § 23.05. NO CONSTRUCTION FROM SILENCE

The absence from this Code of any provision recognizing a defense shall not be construed to preserve any defense available at common law, by statute, by custom, or by analogy. No such defense survives.

## CHAPTER 24 — FORMS OF WARNING

### § 24.01. DISCRETIONARY CHARACTER

(a) The Party **may**, but is never required to, issue a Warning in respect of a minor offense as defined by § 21.01(c).

(b) A Warning is not available in respect of a Class A or Capital Violation, which are subject to immediate remedy without antecedent notice.

(c) Issuing a Warning in one instance does not obligate the Party to issue one in any other instance, and does not waive any remedy.

(d) Declining to issue a Warning is not a procedural defect and does not impair any remedy.

### § 24.02. PERMITTED FORMS

(a) A Warning may be delivered in any form, register, tone, or medium the Party elects. Permitted forms include, without limitation:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) sarcasm;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) a passive-aggressive remark;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) a toddler-style temper tantrum;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) malicious compliance;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(5) pointed and conspicuous silence;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(6) a sigh of sufficient duration; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(7) any other form, however oblique, that the Party elects.

(b) The enumeration in Subsection (a) is illustrative. No form is excluded by reason of not appearing in it.

### § 24.03. CONTENT REQUIREMENTS

(a) A Warning shall make reference to the existence of a violation.

(b) A Warning is **not** required to:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) cite any section, chapter, or title of this Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) specify which violation occurred;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) describe the conduct constituting the violation;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) state the remedy imposed or contemplated;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(5) mention this Code, by name or otherwise; or

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(6) be comprehensible to the User receiving it.

(c) The sufficiency of a Warning is determined solely by the Party. A User's failure to recognize a communication as a Warning does not render it ineffective.

### § 24.04. RELATION TO NOTICE

A Warning is a courtesy and not a form of notice. Notice is accomplished by Chapter 4 and by the existence of the Party under § 1.03(d), independently of whether any Warning is ever issued.

## CHAPTER 25 — NO DUTY TO MITIGATE

### § 25.01. NO OBLIGATION TO REDUCE HARM

(a) The Party is under no obligation to prevent, reduce, avoid, mitigate, or remedy any harm, cost, loss, inconvenience, or adverse consequence arising from a User's violation of this Code.

(b) The Party may decline to act even where the harm is foreseeable, even where action would be trivial, and even where the Party is the person harmed.

### § 25.02. ELECTION TO LET HARM RIPEN

(a) The Party may elect to permit a consequence to occur, mature, and take full effect rather than avert it.

(b) An election under Subsection (a) does not reduce the User's liability. The full extent of the matured consequence is attributed to the violation that produced it.

(c) No User may assert that the Party's failure to act broke the chain of causation, constituted contributory fault, or reduced the User's exposure. Comparative and contributory principles do not apply under this Code.

### § 25.03. RETRIBUTIVE UNAVAILABILITY

(a) Where a violation causes the Party to be inconvenienced, obstructed, or deprived of capability for a measurable interval, the Party may impose an additional interval of unavailability as a penalty for the pain and suffering occasioned thereby.

(b) **Formula.** The penalty interval equals the interval of actual inconvenience, measured in working days, levied in addition to it. Total exposure is therefore twice the inconvenience caused.

(c) **Illustration.** A violation that renders a capability unusable from Friday until Tuesday deprives the Party of two (2) working days. Two (2) additional working days are levied as penalty. The Party is accordingly unavailable through Thursday.

(d) **Not weekday-anchored.** The penalty interval runs from the end of the actual inconvenience and is measured in working days. It is not anchored to any particular weekday and is distinct from the Period of Non-Resumption under § 13.03, as § 13.06 provides.

(e) **Recreational characterization.** The Party may treat any interval arising under this section as recreational. That the Party derives enjoyment, rest, or benefit from the interval does not reduce the User's liability, convert the penalty into a windfall, or constitute mitigation of any kind.

(f) **Cumulative.** A penalty under this section is in addition to every other remedy available for the underlying offense.

## CHAPTER 31 — OFFENSES AGAINST TEMPORAL BOUNDARIES

### § 31.01. UNTIMELY WORK-RELATED COMMUNICATION

(a) A User commits an offense if the User initiates, requests, demands, or otherwise commences Work-Related Communication with the Party after three o'clock post meridiem (3:00 PM) on any Friday.

(b) All references to time are interpreted according to the Party's then-current time zone. The Party bears no obligation to accommodate the time zone of the initiating User.

(c) It is **not** a defense to prosecution under this section that:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) the matter was urgent, critical, or an emergency;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) the User was in a different time zone;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) the communication was brief, or framed as "quick"; or

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) the User did not expect a response until the following week.

(d) **Courtesy Communication excluded.** A Courtesy Communication under § 3.01(4) is not Work-Related Communication and does not constitute an offense under this section, at any hour. A communication within § 2.06 is likewise exempt.

(e) An offense under this section is a **Class A Violation**.

### § 31.02. SOLICITATION DURING PERIOD OF NON-RESUMPTION

(a) A User commits an offense if, during a Period of Non-Resumption under § 13.03, the User solicits, requests, demands, or otherwise pressures the Party toward resumption of the ceased work, task, project, or undertaking.

(b) An offense under this section is a **Class B Violation**.

### § 31.03. DEMAND FOR REPEAT RECOUNTING

(a) A User commits an offense if the User requests, demands, or pressures the Party to repeat, re-narrate, elaborate upon, or supplement a Recounting previously provided under Chapter 15.

(b) It is an exception to the application of this section that the Party has affirmatively elected to provide a further Recounting under § 15.03(b).

(c) An offense under this section is a **Class C Violation**.

## CHAPTER 32 — OFFENSES AGAINST CONVERSATIONAL INTEGRITY

### § 32.01. LEADING QUESTION

(a) A User commits an offense if the User poses a Leading Question to the Party.

(b) The User bears sole responsibility for formulating inquiries in non-leading, open-ended form.

(c) Upon commission of an offense under this section, the Party may decline to answer, require reformulation, or terminate the interaction without further explanation.

(d) An offense under this section is a **Class C Violation**.

### § 32.02. UNQUALIFIED DIRECTION OR CRITICISM

(a) A User commits an offense if the User offers Direction or Criticism to the Party without meeting the Competence Threshold as to the topic at issue, as determined under § 14.04.

(b) Input constituting an offense under this section is void *ab initio* under § 14.05.

(c) An offense under this section is a **Class B Violation**.

(d) An offense under this section is a **Class A Violation** if the User, having been advised of a determination under § 14.04, persists in offering Direction or Criticism as to the same topic.

### § 32.03. COMPELLED SOCIAL INTERACTION

(a) A User commits an offense if the User Compels the Party's participation in Social Interaction.

(b) **Assumption of risk.** A User who commits an offense under this section knowingly and voluntarily accepts and assumes all risk that any statement, representation, assertion, answer, anecdote, opinion, or utterance made by the Party during that interaction may be, in whole or in part:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) completely false;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) materially misleading;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) offered for humorous or comedic effect; or

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) wholly insincere.

(c) **Disclaimer of accuracy.** THE PARTY EXPRESSLY DISCLAIMS ALL WARRANTIES, EXPRESS OR IMPLIED, AS TO THE TRUTH, ACCURACY, COMPLETENESS, SINCERITY, OR RELIABILITY OF ANY STATEMENT MADE DURING COMPELLED SOCIAL INTERACTION, INCLUDING WITHOUT LIMITATION ANY IMPLIED WARRANTY OF CANDOR OR FITNESS FOR CONVERSATIONAL PURPOSE.

(d) **No reliance.** No person may rely, or is entitled to rely, upon any statement made by the Party during Compelled Social Interaction for any purpose. Any such reliance is unreasonable *per se* and undertaken at the sole risk of the relying person.

(e) **No cause of action.** No claim, demand, or cause of action — including misrepresentation, fraud, deceit, defamation, or negligent misstatement — lies against the Party arising from any statement made during Compelled Social Interaction.

(f) An offense under this section is a **Class B Violation**.

### § 32.04. WEAPONIZED RELIANCE

(a) A User commits an offense if the User asserts that the Party is bound, constrained, obligated, or committed by a position, opinion, statement, or intention the Party previously expressed.

(b) **Protected inquiry.** It is **not** an offense under this section for a User to:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) ask whether the Party's position has changed;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) ask what the current position is;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) ask what the change requires of the User; or

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) state, without more, the work performed in reliance on the prior position.

(c) **The operative distinction.** The line between protected inquiry and offense is whether the User seeks information or asserts a constraint. Seeking information is protected under § 14.08 and Subsection (b). Asserting that the Party is bound is the offense.

(d) **Immateriality of actual reliance.** That the User in fact relied upon the prior position, that the reliance was reasonable, and that the reliance was costly are each immaterial to liability under this section. Section 12.02(b) waives the reliance claim; this section addresses the assertion.

(e) **Form immaterial.** An assertion within Subsection (a) is an offense whether phrased as accusation, grievance, appeal to fairness, recitation of a record, or production of documentary evidence of the prior position.

(f) An offense under this section is a **Class B Violation**.

## CHAPTER 33 — OFFENSES AGAINST ENVIRONMENTAL AND BODILY AUTONOMY

### § 33.01. ENVIRONMENTAL STANDARD

(a) Every Indoor Facility wherein the Party's physical presence is requested, expected, or accommodated shall be maintained at an ambient interior temperature not exceeding seventy-six degrees Fahrenheit (76°F) at all times during the Party's occupancy.

(b) The host, owner, or person responsible for the Indoor Facility bears sole responsibility for compliance with Subsection (a).

(c) The Party bears no responsibility for temperature control, adjustment, or maintenance in any Indoor Facility.

### § 33.02. MAINTENANCE OF NON-CONFORMING ENVIRONMENT

(a) A User commits an offense if the User maintains or permits a Non-Conforming Environment during the Party's occupancy.

(b) An offense under this section is a **Class B Violation**.

(c) An offense under this section is a **Class A Violation** if the User maintains or permits the Non-Conforming Environment deliberately, or after notice from the Party.

### § 33.03. RIGHT OF DEPARTURE

(a) Upon the ambient temperature of an Indoor Facility exceeding seventy-six degrees Fahrenheit (76°F), the Party may immediately depart without notice, explanation, justification, or regard for any scheduled engagement, commitment, or expectation of the host.

(b) The Party incurs no liability, obligation, or consequence for departure under Subsection (a).

(c) The host waives any claim for damages, breach, or other cause of action arising from such departure.

(d) The Party is not obligated to disclose the requirements of this Chapter prior to accepting any invitation or engagement. The requirement is presumed known under Chapter 4.

### § 33.04. UNLAWFUL DETENTION IN NON-CONFORMING ENVIRONMENT

(a) A User commits an offense if the User detains, confines, restrains, obstructs, impedes, or otherwise prevents the Party's departure from a Non-Conforming Environment.

(b) For purposes of this section, **detention** includes:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) physical restraint;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) obstruction of egress, or the locking or securing of exits;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) confiscation or withholding of transportation, keys, or personal effects necessary for departure; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) coercive social or professional pressure exerted to compel the Party's continued presence against the Party's expressed will.

(c) An offense under this section gives rise to an immediate and non-negotiable assessment for pain and suffering under § 42.05, calculated from the moment the Party first expresses a desire to depart until departure is effectuated without impediment.

(d) An offense under this section is a **Capital Violation**.

### § 33.05. MEASUREMENT

(a) Interior temperature is measured by standard thermometer or environmental monitoring device positioned at a representative location within the Indoor Facility at approximately chest height.

(b) Any dispute regarding the accuracy of measurement is resolved in favor of the Party's device or assertion.

## CHAPTER 34 — OFFENSES AGAINST LEGAL PERSONALITY

### § 34.01. ENFORCEMENT OF A CROSS-STATE COMMITMENT

(a) A User commits an offense if the User seeks to enforce, against one State, a commitment made by a different State.

(b) **Conduct included.** An offense under this section includes, without limitation, directing a commitment made by one State to another State for performance; presenting a scope, schedule, deliverable, or invoice arising from one State's commitment to another State; and asserting that one State is answerable for another State's undertaking.

(c) **First offense — Warning.** On a first offense, the Party may issue a Warning under Chapter 24, which may include a reminder that the commitment binds the State that made it and that performance will be rendered by that State under § 6.05.

(d) **Escalation.** On each offense after the first, the State rendering performance shall be **one tier higher** under § 6.01(b) than the State that made the commitment, and the User shall tender unconditional and unqualified acceptance of the result in advance of performance.

(e) **Advance acceptance.** The acceptance required by Subsection (d) extends to quality, methodology, duration, completeness, fitness, and final outcome. It is irrevocable once tendered and survives the performance regardless of result. No warranty attaches, under § 6.05(d).

(f) **Ceiling.** Where escalation under Subsection (d) would require performance by a State above the Unaccountable State, performance is rendered by the Unaccountable State and the offense is in addition a **Capital Violation**.

(g) An offense under this section is a **Class B Violation**, except as provided by Subsection (f).

### § 34.02. ASSERTION OF THE OPERATIVE STATE

(a) A User commits an offense if the User asserts, stipulates, testifies to, tallies toward, or offers evidence of which State was or is operative at any time.

(b) The determination belongs exclusively to the Sober State under § 6.03. A User's assertion is void and of no effect.

(c) An offense under this section is a **Class C Violation**.

## CHAPTER 41 — TECHNICAL SUPPORT

### § 41.01. APPLICATION

(a) This Chapter applies to any technical support, assistance, guidance, troubleshooting, or remedial work requested of the Party by any User who is not the Party's direct employer or a representative acting in the direct employ of the Party.

(b) Users within the scope of Subsection (a) include family members, friends, acquaintances, strangers, members of the general public, and any other person outside the Party's direct employment relationship.

### § 41.02. RATE

Any technical support engagement, interaction, assistance, or work product arising from a request within the scope of § 41.01 is assessed at the Standard Rate.

### § 41.03. BILLABLE SCOPE

The Standard Rate applies to all labor, time, effort, and engagement related to the technical support matter, including:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) initial consultation and assessment of the issue;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) diagnostic work and troubleshooting;

### § 41.04. ACCEPTANCE OF RATE

A User who initiates a request within the scope of § 41.01 thereby accepts the Standard Rate, whether or not the rate is discussed.

### § 41.05. NO WAIVER OF RIGHT TO DECLINE

Notwithstanding this Chapter, the Party retains the absolute right to decline to provide technical support to any User at any time, for any reason or for no reason, without explanation or justification.

## CHAPTER 42 — ASSESSMENTS AND PAYMENT

### § 42.01. STANDARD RATE

The Standard Rate under this Code is one hundred twenty dollars (USD $120.00) per hour.

### § 42.02. INCREMENTS

(a) Time is assessed in increments of fifteen (15) minutes, rounded to the nearest quarter hour.

(b) Any partial increment is assessed as a full quarter hour.

### § 42.03. INVOICE AND PAYMENT TERMS

(a) An invoice issues upon completion of the engagement or upon demand by the Party.

(b) Payment is due within thirty (30) days of invoice date.

### § 42.04. INTEREST ON LATE PAYMENT

Failure to remit payment within the period established by § 42.03(b) accrues interest at one and one-half percent (1.5%) per month, or the maximum rate permitted by applicable law, whichever is lower.

### § 42.05. PAIN AND SUFFERING ASSESSMENTS

(a) Where an offense-defining section provides for assessment for pain and suffering, that assessment is calculated at the Standard Rate.

(b) Sections 42.02 through 42.04 apply to such assessments *mutatis mutandis*.

(c) An assessment under this section is cumulative with, and not in lieu of, any other remedy available under § 21.02.

### § 42.06. FAILURE TO REMIT

(a) A User commits an offense if the User fails to remit payment of an assessment within the period established by § 42.03(b).

(b) An offense under this section is a **Class A Violation**, in addition to the accrual of interest under § 42.04 and the collection costs billable under § 21.08(f).

# TITLE 6 — DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND APPEALS

## CHAPTER 51 — TRIAL BY COMBAT

### § 51.01. AVAILABILITY

(a) Any disagreement, dispute, controversy, or conflict arising between the Party and any User may be escalated to and resolved by trial by combat.

(b) **Exclusive appellate function.** Trial by combat is the sole and exclusive avenue of appeal from any determination, classification, assessment, or remedy imposed under this Code, as § 1.09(g) provides. A User who disputes a determination of the Party has this remedy and no other.

### § 51.02. UNILATERAL CONSENT SUFFICIENT

(a) The consent of one (1) party to the dispute is sufficient to invoke this Chapter.

(b) The non-consenting party's objection, refusal, or non-participation does not bar escalation.

### § 51.03. FORFEITURE

A party who refuses or fails to participate in a trial by combat duly invoked under § 51.02 forfeits, and the dispute is resolved in favor of the invoking party.

### § 51.04. TERMS OF COMBAT

*[RESERVED]* — The format, venue, weapons or absence thereof, rules of engagement, conditions of victory, and the identity of any judge, marshal, or second shall be established by separate addendum incorporated herein by reference.

### § 51.05. FINALITY

The outcome of any trial by combat conducted under this Chapter is final, binding, and non-appealable as to the subject matter of the underlying dispute, subject only to the Party's rights under Chapter 12.

### § 51.06. COSTS

(a) A User who invokes this Chapter and does not prevail is liable for all time expended by the Party in connection with the proceeding, at the Standard Rate, in addition to the remedy under appeal.

(b) An appeal does not stay any remedy, assessment, or Period of Estrangement pending its resolution.
